Two weeks of warning, so residents keep the caregivers who know them.

Nurselytix forecasts nurse staffing shortfalls in nursing homes 2 to 4 weeks ahead, so directors of nursing can fill gaps with familiar staff before understaffing reaches residents.

Status. In development. Models are validated on public data; a pilot with Wisconsin nursing homes is forming now.

Two weeks out

Four weeks out

Pace admissions to the staff you will have. Start recruiting for the gap.

The gap nobody saw coming, measured

Agency nurses are who a nursing home calls when its own staff cannot cover a shift, often people who have never met the residents. The share of hours they work is the measurable trace of shortfalls residents lived through.

Wisconsin's agency share was flat near 2 to 3% in 2017 and 2018, began rising in 2019, and peaked after the pandemic, not during it.

Why it matters for residents

When a home runs short, the people who feel it first are frail older adults, many living with dementia, who depend on caregivers who know their routines, their risks and their baselines. A thin shift or an unfamiliar caregiver is where care slips.

That is why Nurselytix aims at the weeks before a shortfall, while a director of nursing can still put familiar staff on the shift. Our next research step is examining how understaffed periods relate to falls, pressure injuries and hospital transfers in CMS quality data.

Work in progress. The link between our warnings and resident outcomes is something we are studying, not a result we claim yet.

What public data alone can already predict

Before any home shared data with us, we tested whether federal staffing records could warn of agency-staffing spikes ahead of time. They can. Agency spikes are our first measurable signal; next we model the understaffing residents experience directly.

Spike: a week when a home's agency use jumps well above its own recent normal.

Weekends are where the gaps open

Across 530,000 facility-days in Wisconsin, hands-on nursing hours per resident drop on Saturday and Sunday, and agency hours rise to fill them.

Part of the weekend dip is planned staffing, not a shortfall. Night shifts are not visible in public data; they come with each home's own schedules.

How it works

  1. A risk score on day one

    Every Medicare- and Medicaid-certified nursing home already reports daily staffing to the federal government. Nurselytix uses those records to score a home before it shares anything; turnover, occupancy and ownership data come next.

  2. Your weekly schedule data sharpens it

    Each week the home sends standard exports of census, schedules, time off and call-outs. No resident health records are needed.

  3. A weekly warning for the director of nursing

    Uncovered RN, LPN and CNA hours by unit, shift and role, 2 to 4 weeks ahead, with a range to plan against and the likely fixes.
